# Hiking Boots Guide

This guide covers the Trail Hiking Boots, waterproof leather boots with ankle support and a grippy sole.

## How should hiking boots fit?
Hiking boots should leave about a thumb's width of space (roughly 1 cm) in front of your longest toe, so your toes do not hit the front on downhill sections. Your heel should stay in place without lifting more than a few millimetres when you walk. Try boots on in the afternoon, when feet are slightly swollen, and wear the socks you will hike in.

## What if I have wide feet?
Try the Trail Hiking Boots with mid-weight hiking socks and loosen the laces over the forefoot. If your toes feel squeezed when the boots are laced normally, choose a larger size and check that your heel still stays in place.

## How do I break in new boots?
Wear new boots indoors for 2 to 3 hours a day for a week, then on short walks of 3 to 5 km before a long hike. Never wear brand new boots on a long trek.

## How do I stop blisters?
Stop when you feel a hot spot and cover it with a blister plaster before it becomes a blister. Use socks made of merino wool, keep them dry, and tie the laces firmly around the ankle so your heel does not rub.

## How do I clean and waterproof my boots?
Brush off dry mud, then wipe the boots with a damp cloth and lukewarm water. Dry them at room temperature, away from heaters and direct sun. Apply a leather wax or conditioner every 2 to 3 months of regular use to keep the leather water resistant.
